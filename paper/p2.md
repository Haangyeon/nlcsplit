## Abstract

The VV10 nonlocal-correlation term of omegaB97X-V is evaluated as a double sum over
real-space integration points, so its cost is quadratic in the number of points. We
implemented a *certified* screening scheme for it: every pair of grid-point blocks is
either evaluated exactly or replaced by an upper bound proved to dominate its
contribution, the bounds accumulate into one pre-declared error, and the point-pair
structure is kept intact so the result can still be attributed fragment by fragment. We
then measured the scheme against the dense sum under a recorded contention gate, and
report that it does not pay. At the accuracy a fragment-pair decomposition needs
(1e-6 kcal/mol per pair term) the bound is so loose that 84.4-95.4% of the far-field
point pairs must still be evaluated; of the 33 screened-versus-exact pairs sampled in two
independent timing windows on one machine, none was faster than the dense sum, and the
largest slowdown measured is 13.29x. Three measurements explain the outcome and are the
substance of this paper. At atom-block granularity the *smallest* block-pair bound in a
real dimer exceeds the whole quantity it is supposed to judge by a factor of 2.4e5. Every
root box separation in the dimers studied is exactly 0.0000 A, so the near field is 100%
of the cross point pairs and a geometric accept/reject rule has a 0.0% saving ceiling.
Tightening the bound by splitting each block into a dense core and a sparse tail raises
the fraction of droppable point pairs only from 12.349% to 13.650%. One criterion that
looks obviously monotone under refinement - a mass-weighted ball - loses the property on
18% of refinements, found only by counting. We state the four conditions a certified
scheme would have to meet before it could win, note that none holds for weakly bound
dimers, and keep the screening off by default.

## 1. What we built, what it measured, and what was already known

*Screening* in this paper is an algorithmic sparsity truncation with a proof attached: a
block pair is skipped only when an upper bound on its contribution, computed before the
skip, dominates everything that would have been lost. It is not the Lindhard/dielectric
screening that the vdW-DF literature also calls "screening", and we avoid that reading by
never calling the kernel screened — only the sum is.

The VV10 nonlocal-correlation energy of ωB97X-V is evaluated as a bilinear form over a
real-space integration grid, and the cost is quadratic in the number of retained grid
points. We built a scheme that keeps the exact value for the block pairs it evaluates and
carries a **certified** upper bound for the ones it drops, accumulated into a single
pre-declared `cert_err`, while preserving the grid-point-pair structure that a
fragment-pair energy decomposition needs (P1's quantity). Then we measured it, and the
measurement is the paper:

- at the accuracy the decomposition requires (1e-6 kcal/mol on a fragment-pair term), the
  screened path still evaluates **84.4-95.4%** of the far-field point pairs;
- with no other runnable user task at the sampling instant (external R-state count 0;
  `load1` is recorded, 0.99-4.24, but the core count is not, so this is a contention gate
  and **not** a claim that the box was idle), across 24 timed runs in the archived window and
  20 in a second independent one — 33 screened/exact pairs in total — the screened path is
  **1.08-10.28× slower** in the first window and **1.37-13.29×** in the second, never faster;
  at the tightest tolerance tested (τ = 1e-12 Ha), counting every row at that tolerance, the penalty is
  **1.95-10.28×** and **2.63-13.29×** (**1.95-10.85×** over the eight rows that meet the target),
  and even there 3 of the 11 timed rows still miss the 1e-6 kcal/mol target: 1.8e-6 in the
  12-atom level-0 row of each window, 1.5e-6 in the 9-atom level-1 row of the archived one
  (§6);
- the reason is visible in the bound itself: at atom-block granularity the *smallest*
  block-pair bound in a real water dimer exceeds the entire quantity it is meant to judge
  by a factor of **2.4e5**, and the near field accounts for **100%** of the cross
  point-pairs — every root box separation is exactly 0.0000 Å (§3, §5).

**What is not new.** Making this double sum cheaper is a live target. VV10 itself was
introduced as a closed-form kernel replacing the adiabatic-connection integrand
[[vydrov2009vv10]], with a separable/efficient form soon after
[[vydrov2010simple]]; [[sabatini2013efficient]],
and real-space grid implementations with parallel post-processing exist [[lazic2010junolo]]. The nearest current work is
Laikov's preprint
"Nonlocal van der Waals density functional made faster"
[[laikov2026faster]] (v2, 2026-01-19),
which *approximates the six-dimensional integrand by products of three-dimensional
density-like distributions and potential-like functions of the interelectronic distance*,
explicitly to open it to fast-multipole evaluation, with a Gaussian-fitted analytic variant
on top; the claimed accuracy is empirical — its own noble-gas-dimer table gives a well-depth
deviation of 103.6 µEh for XeXe, and its text assesses the tuned variant as "below 18 %" for
bond energies (mostly below 1.2 % for bond lengths) — no tolerance parameter and no bound
are given, and the pairwise accumulation structure is not what the method returns. Read end to end, it is a
*kernel replacement*: an empirically tuned separable rational expansion standing in for
VV10's Φ, which is the right move when only the total is wanted. We screen the original sum,
which is the only move available when the answer must be a pair-by-pair attribution — and we
measure what preserving it costs. Efficient real-space evaluation of a
nonlocal term is also established for periodic codes [[nabok2011noloco]] and in the vdW-DF
grid implementations cited
above. Hierarchical box/MAC machinery with provable geometric error bounds is classical for
Coulomb integrals and exact exchange [[schwegler1998multipole]]; [[dehnen2002hierarchical]]. The abbreviation "JCP" is deliberately spelled out in both
halves of that pair: the two papers sit in different journals whose initials collide, and a
reader who goes looking in one finds nothing. The closest *mechanism* neighbour is not a
nonlocal-term paper at all: the "Linear Scaling Hierarchical Integration Scheme for the
Exchange-Correlation Term" of Burow & Sierka [[burow2011hierarchical]] octrees the grid and
screens negligible contributions adaptively — for a *single* integral over a semi-local
functional, with no rigorous bound and no pair attribution. The "have a proof" half of our conjunction
likewise has ancestry outside this object: rigorous multipole upper bounds for
electron-repulsion integrals [[lambrecht2005estimates]]; [[lambrecht2005screening]]; [[doser2008tighter]] and a
rigorous relative-error criterion for tree/FMM summation of Newton potentials [[engblom2011wellseparated]]. The object is what is unclaimed: ∬ ρ Φ ρ with VV10's Φ,
screened by a proven bound, with the pair ledger intact.

**Publishing a refutation is not new either**, and we say so deliberately: the
"which fast scheme is actually faster at molecular sizes" comparison is a mature genre in
electrostatics and N-body work [[luty1994pppmesh]]; [[petersen1995pmesh]]; [[deserno1998meshup]]; [[gumerov2008fmmgpu]]; [[kohnke2020gmxfmm]],
where the crossover size is known to depend on the kernel and the constants rather than on
the asymptotic exponent. Our result is that same shape of measurement for a different
bilinear form, and we cite these as methodological neighbours rather than as prior art on
VV10.

**Scope, and how confident the novelty statement is.** We claim: (i) a certified per-block-
pair upper bound for the VV10 real-space double sum, (ii) that it is implemented without
destroying the pair attribution, and (iii) the quantitative cost measurement of (i)+(ii)
against the dense kernel. We do not claim the first hierarchical or fast-multipole treatment
of a nonlocal correlation term. The literature screen behind those sentences is
`RESEARCH-20260930-p2-novelty.md`: 44 Web-of-Science Core Collection search strings executed
through the site's own record endpoint with six known-item calibrations passing, plus
Crossref, OpenAlex and arXiv, every string and hit count logged. Its limits are stated in the
same file and are not small: three of the five citation-hub sweeps attempted (Sabatini, with
738 records, Gulans with 167, JuNoLo with 47) were cut off by HTTP 429 at the first page and
returned nothing, while the two that completed were the Laikov hub (0 citations, so no
indexed follow-up exists yet) and the Román-Pérez & Soler hub (1,596 fetched against a
server count of 1,590, reconciliation passed, one title hit and it is a review); a sixth hub

of the implementation papers - where an empirical cutoff would be documented - was read only

collision is a journal version of Laikov's preprint adding an error analysis, and this

## 2. Setting

The nonlocal-correlation term of a VV10-type functional is a double sum over numerical
integration points,

    E_NL = 1/2 Σ_i Σ_j w_i ρ_i w_j ρ_j Φ(g_ij, g'_ij),      Φ = −3 / (2 g g' (g + g'))

with `g = w0(r_j) R_ij² + κ(r_i)` under same-centre pairing, `w0 = sqrt(C|∇ρ|⁴/ρ⁴ +
(4π/3)ρ)` (σ = |∇ρ|², so σ² enters) and `κ = b(3π/2)(ρ/9π)^{1/6}` (b = 6.0, C = 0.01)
[`nlcsplit/nlc.py:31-43`; the pairing convention and its cost are established in P1 §2.1]. The reported quantity
adds the local part `E_loc = β∫ρ`, `β = (1/32)(3/b²)^{3/4}` [`nlcsplit/nlc.py:112`].

Two properties of this sum decide everything below.

**(i) There is no sign cancellation inside a block.** ρ ≥ 0, the integration weights enter
through `wρ`, and Φ < 0 everywhere, so a block sum is bounded by the sum of absolute
values without loss [`nlcsplit/fastpair.py:17-18`]. Using `m_P = Σ_{i∈P}|w_i ρ_i|` is
therefore not a looseness introduced for convenience — it is the only quantity the
integrands control.

**(ii) The attribution structure is the product, not the sum.** We keep `pairs[(A,B)]`
for atom (or fragment) blocks P, Q, so `E_pair = Σ_a v(a,a) + 2 Σ_{a<b} v(a,b)` and the
factor of two is applied exactly once, at block level [`nlcsplit/tests/test_fastpair.py::test_x2_not_applied_twice`;
the half-block convention of `nlc.pair_energy` is documented at `nlcsplit/step_d_floor.py:18`].
A point-value kernel evaluation returns `dE_NLC` and nothing else; the pair structure is
what a screening scheme has to preserve to be usable for the attribution question P1 asks.

**Two independent truncations, reported separately.** The density floor `RHO_FLOOR = 1e-12`
[`nlcsplit/nlc.py:27`] already discards points before any geometry is consulted: on the water
dimer at grid level 1 it keeps 17,227 of 19,936 points
[`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out`, line 3; the same two counts appear
as `n_grid` and `n_kept` in `evidence/fastpair_ab/fastpair_ab_compare.json`]. What the floor
alone costs is what `nlcsplit/step_d_floor.py` measures. Screening is a second, orthogonal
truncation. Conflating them would make the certified error meaningless, so every number below
is reported with the floor held fixed and the screening budget accounted separately.

## 3. A certified block-pair bound

For a block pair (P, Q) with axis-aligned bounding boxes, let `D` be the box separation
(`D = 0` when the boxes overlap) and

    a_P = w0_min(P)·D² + κ_min(P),      bound(P,Q) = 0.75·m_P·m_Q / (a_P a_Q (a_P + a_Q)).

Then `|Σ_{i∈P} Σ_{j∈Q} w_i ρ_i w_j ρ_j Φ_ij| ≤ bound(P,Q)`, and for a same-level block pair
the ×2 convention gives `mult = 2.0`. It is applied once, at the `_mac` call site inside
`nlcsplit/fastpair.py::screened_decomposition`; the accept/reject recursion itself is
`nlcsplit/fastpair.py::_mac`, and the bound function is
`nlcsplit/fastpair.py::node_bound`.

The bound is not tight by construction — it is tight *enough to be the thing we measure*.
On real water-dimer fields the worst ratio over 390 sampled leaf pairs is
`bound/|actual| = 1.181e+07` for the whole-block bound
[`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out`, Q2c, lines 134 and 147] —
a factor above ten to the seventh power, which is the honest starting point for §4–§6.

**What a global minimum does not buy.** A bound built from one global `w0_min`/`κ_min` is
useless rather than merely loose, and how useless it is gets fixed by the density floor, not
by any one measurement. Every retained point has `rho > 1e-12` [`nlcsplit/nlc.py:27`], and at
that floor with `σ = 0` the §2 formulas give
`κ_min = 6.0·(3π/2)·(1e-12/9π)^{1/6} = 0.162` and
`w0_min = sqrt((4π/3)·1e-12) = 2.05e-6` [`nlcsplit/nlc.py:31-43`]. No grid at this floor can
supply a smaller pair: `κ` grows with `ρ`, and `w0` is at least `sqrt((4π/3)ρ)` because its
gradient term is non-negative. A bound built on one global pair therefore has to be ready for
the R⁻⁶ asymptotic regime to begin only at 281 bohr ≈ 149 Å, far beyond any separation in the
systems here (one line by hand: `sqrt(kappa_min/w0_min) = sqrt(0.162/2.05e-6) = 281.1` bohr,
and 281.1 × 0.529177 = 148.7 Å). Those two literals are recorded in `PROGRESS-nlcsplit.md` as
a design instruction, not in any log, which is why they are re-derived above rather than
cited to a file. What the water dimer at level 1 measures per atom block is `κ` 0.163-0.193
and `w0` 0.269-0.313 [lines 64-69 of
`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out`]: `κ` is
already at its floor limit, and one number for the whole grid would price every pair at the
sparsest atom in it. Per-block minima are therefore mandatory, and what does the pruning is
the density mass `m_P`, not the distance.

**`screen` is an acceptance constant, not a budget.** τ is tested per node pair, so the
accumulated guarantee is `cert_err = Σ accepted bounds`, and it is `cert_err` — not τ —
that must be compared with an accuracy requirement. On the benzene sandwich at level 0,
taking every node pair whose individual bound is below 1e-8 Ha accepts 118,250 of them and
the accumulated bound is 4.443e-4 Ha = 0.279 kcal/mol, four orders of magnitude above the
per-pair threshold one was checking against
[`evidence/bound_forms/20260929T101447Z_probe_bound_forms.out`, benzene block, `tau=1e-08`
row]. A test pins the budget reading: requiring `cert_err ≤ 1e-9 Ha` selects τ = 1e-13 and
reproduces the inter-fragment term to 2.763e-8 kcal/mol (−0.523653418 against
−0.523653446)
[`nlcsplit/tests/test_fastpair.py::test_L2_fragment_term_survives_screening`, whose stdout
line is archived at `evidence/fastpair_ab/20260929T105823Z_pytest_fastpair_L2_L4.out`].

## 4. Which geometric criterion can screen at all

A top-down accept/reject is sound only if refining a block never *increases* its bound:
accepting a parent pair must over-estimate the total of everything it replaces. This is a
property of the geometric representation, and it is testable, so we tested it.

| representation | separation used | monotonicity violations / 300,000 random refinements |
|---|---|---|
| mass-weighted ball (centroid + max radius) | `D = max(0, |c_P − c_Q| − r_P − r_Q)` | **53,983 (18.0%)** |
| axis-aligned box | per-axis gap, Euclidean norm | **0** |

[`nlcsplit/tests/test_fastpair.py::test_box_separation_is_never_increased_by_refinement`,
`...::test_ball_bound_can_lose_monotonicity_under_refinement`; counts recorded in
`PROGRESS-nlcsplit.md` §"geometry change rationale"]

The ball failure is not numerical noise; it has a four-point hand-computable counterexample
(two equal-mass point sets whose parent balls are 7.929 apart while a child pair is 5.0
apart, because a child ball protrudes outside its parent) and it is pinned as a test. The
mechanism is that a mass-weighted centroid with a maximum-radius bound is not the minimum
enclosing ball, so refinement can enlarge the covered region.

**This is a negative result at the level of the criterion, independent of cost.** A ball
criterion cannot be used for top-down screening at any tolerance and on any hardware, and
we initially believed the opposite: the claim that balls are "obviously" monotone under
refinement was made in prose before it was counted, and the count refuted it. We state it
because it is the methodological point of this section: *the choice of geometric
representation for a certified scheme must be settled by a refinement-monotonicity count,
not by an inclusion intuition.*

## 5. Where the tightness actually comes from

Box and ball agree at the top of the hierarchy, because at the root every separation is
zero: the probe prints one figure in both its `ball_bnd` and `box_bnd` columns for all 15
water-dimer pairs, sum 1.363e+07 kcal/mol each way, "box is 1.0e+00x tighter"
[`evidence/bound_forms/20260929T101447Z_probe_bound_forms.out`]. For the water dimer the 15
root-pair sums come out bit-identical under the two criteria, 21723.206496881914 Ha each,
and that is why the test which once asserted `box < ball` now asserts the cause instead:
both root separations are zero, pair by pair
[`nlcsplit/tests/test_fastpair.py::test_ball_control_drops_nothing`]. The archived A/B run
records the same box sum as 21723.206496881437 Ha, bit-identical between its unscreened row
and its τ = 1e-8 row, but 4.8e-13 Ha below the test value
[`evidence/fastpair_ab/fastpair_ab_compare.json`, `sum_atomblock_root_bound_ha`]. So
"bit-identical" is on record within a run; across the two runs the same quantity agrees
to 2e-17 relative. At the root nothing is usable: for the benzene sandwich at level 0, **0 of
276 atom pairs** have a root box bound below 1e-8 Ha, against an exact inter-fragment total
of 38.3510 kcal/mol [`evidence/bound_forms/20260929T101447Z_probe_bound_forms.out`].

The cause is geometric and it is the same in every system we ran: low-density tail points
inflate each atom's bounding box until neighbouring boxes overlap, so `D = 0` at the root
and the distance term contributes nothing. On the water dimer all 15 cross-atom root pairs
have a box gap of exactly 0.0000 Å, and for benzene 276/276
[`evidence/bound_forms/20260929T112656Z_hybrid_nearfield.out`, "# 276/276 cross atom pairs
print root box gap 0.00 A"]. That log also carries wall-clock rows; every one of them is
marked `valid=False` by our own load gate (they were taken with ~10 external cores busy),
so nothing in §6 comes from it — only the geometric counts. Tightness therefore arrives
only by descending into blocks small enough to be simultaneously dense and compact.

We tested the two ways of getting there.

**Refine below the atom block.** Splitting each atom's retained points by a longest-axis
bisection (stop when mass ≤ 1% of the block *and* radius ≤ 1/3 of the reference, or at
32 points) gives 1,388 nodes and 697 leaves for the water dimer at level 1
[`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out`, line 4], and this is the only
mechanism that fires: at τ = 1e-8 Ha the tree accepts 32,131 node pairs and evaluates 69,003
of the 197,686 leaf pairs, which drops 59.7% of the far point pairs, with `cert_err` =
1.278e-4 Ha ≥ the actual 2.346e-5 Ha
[`evidence/fastpair_ab/fastpair_ab_compare.json`, `ours_tau1em8` rows; the same accepted,
visited and leaf-pair counts are in
`evidence/bound_forms/20260929T101447Z_probe_bound_forms.out`].

**Tighten the bound at fixed granularity.** Splitting each node into a dense core and a
sparse tail and bounding the three mass products separately (`m = m̃ + T`, core-core with
core minima, mixed and tail-tail with the whole-block minima) tightens the *root* bounds by
6.1e3× (2.172e+04 → 3.552 Ha over the 15 root pairs) but the *leaf-pair* sums by only 2.13×
(4.507e-2 → 2.115e-2 Ha), and the number of leaf pairs whose bound clears 1e-12 Ha by
8.8% (59,644 → 64,883) [`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out`, Q2a/Q2b].
Two cautions from the same run: the variant that bounds every tail product by a
distance-free global `sup|Φ|` — the form one of us specified when delegating this
experiment — is **looser than the old bound** on 125,097 of 197,686 leaf pairs; and the
deliberately unsound variant (tail terms using the core `κ̃`) produced zero violations in the
428 real-field groups it was checked over: 228 node pairs, all 15 atom root pairs among
them, plus 200 random point sets. The same run reports zero violations over a further 3,400
synthetic-field criterion evaluations, not broken down by variant. It had to be refuted with
a two-point hand fixture, where it underestimates by 42×. The run says why sampling missed
it: the tail points carry almost no `|w ρ|`, so the products this variant mis-bounds are tiny
in any real sample. Those are the
counts on record, and the 300,000-refinement count belongs to the ball/box
monotonicity test of §4, not to this variant. A negative control with no power is not a test.

## 6. What the screening costs with no other runnable task

Sections 3-5 say the bound is loose and that tightening it at fixed granularity buys
1.11×. Neither of those is a statement about time, so we measured time.

**Protocol.** Water chains of 1-4 monomers (3, 6, 9 and 12 atoms), ωB97X-V/6-31G\*
(cartesian), PySCF 2.12.1 [[sun2020pyscf]], `OMP_NUM_THREADS=4`. For each system we ran the exact
double sum and the same code with screening at τ = 1e-8, 1e-10 and 1e-12 Ha, each after
its own converged SCF, on the same grid. The one thing that made this measurable was a
load gate: a sample is only valid if the number of *Runnable* tasks outside our own
process tree is ≤ 1 at the moment the timed block ends. Two earlier attempts produced
numbers we threw away — one headline ("5.2× slower") was taken while twelve agents were
contending on the box, and before that the gate counted our own work as contention,
which made every sample invalidate itself. All 24 samples in
[`evidence/scaling/step_j.json`] carry `valid_timing: true`.

**Result: the screened path never won, anywhere in the ladder.** Ratios are
screened/exact wall clock, quoted with the fraction of far point pairs the screened path
still evaluated, and the absolute error it made on the pair term:

| system (level 0) | exact (s) | τ=1e-8 | τ=1e-10 | τ=1e-12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 atoms | 0.08 | 5.31×, 72.6%, 1.3e-3 | 6.89×, 88.9%, 1.2e-5 | 7.98×, 95.4%, 6.8e-8 |
| 6 atoms | 0.48 | 2.93×, 55.1%, 7.1e-3 | 4.95×, 81.9%, 4.6e-5 | 5.42×, 93.7%, 1.2e-7 |
| 9 atoms | 0.52 | 5.66×, 46.1%, 1.4e-2 | 8.89×, 74.5%, 1.4e-4 | 10.25×, 91.3%, 6.1e-7 |
| 12 atoms | 0.99 | 4.65×, 37.5%, 2.4e-2 | 8.42×, 66.2%, 3.0e-4 | 10.28×, 86.3%, 1.8e-6 |
| system (level 1) | exact (s) | | | |
| 6 atoms | 4.45 | 1.09×, 41.6%, 1.5e-2 | 1.64×, 68.4%, 7.9e-5 | 1.95×, 84.4%, 4.4e-7 |
| 9 atoms | 9.63 | 1.08×, 31.4%, 3.8e-2 | 1.63×, 60.5%, 3.1e-4 | 2.19×, 79.5%, 1.5e-6 |

Two readings matter more than the headline ratio.

*τ is not a budget.* P1 needs each pair term right to 1e-6 kcal/mol. At τ = 1e-12 the
12-atom level-0 chain is off by 1.8e-6 and the 9-atom level-1 chain by 1.5e-6 — both
outside that, so no single τ in this sweep is admissible across the ladder. The reason is
visible in `cert_err`, the accumulated guarantee, which grows with system size at fixed τ:
2.8e-10 → 1.2e-9 → 3.4e-9 → 9.2e-9 Ha for 3 → 12 atoms at level 0, and 1.4e-8 Ha for
9 atoms at level 1, because it is a sum over accepted node pairs and a bigger chain has
more of them. A per-pair acceptance constant therefore cannot promise a per-quantity
error; only the accumulated bound can, and it is the thing that has to be driven below
target — which pushes τ toward 1e-13, where the MAC simulation in
`evidence/coretail/20260929T130257Z_coretail_bound.out` says only 9.81% of the point
pairs can be dropped with the original bound, and 10.35% at best across every bound
variant it tried there. Over 89% of the far-and-self point pairs are evaluated anyway.

*The traversal is the cost, and it is paid to keep almost all of the work.* The tree
counters for the two level-1 systems show the arithmetic plainly. Take the 9-atom chain at
τ = 1e-12 Ha, the finest rung in its sweep and the only one whose error is anywhere near
the target (1.5e-6 kcal/mol): it visits 391,955 node pairs to decide among 493,395 leaf
pairs, and then evaluates 348,536 of those leaf pairs — 70.6% of them — which is
236,127,947 of 297,165,609 far point pairs, 79.5% of the original work, plus 391,955 bound
evaluations (one per visited node pair, the sidecar's `n_visited`) and a tree of 1,061
leaves built from scratch (`n_leaves = 1061`; the sidecar records leaf
counts for these runs, not a total node count). Skipping 20% of a gemm by doing 400k scalar
bound computations in Python-adjacent numpy is a losing trade at these sizes, and the
loss is largest exactly where the ratio looked best: at level 1 τ = 1e-8 the screened path
is 1.08× slower and wrong by 3.8e-2 kcal/mol.

We deliberately do not plot a scaling curve. An asymptotic claim here would need a
protocol we did not run: `τ` is an absolute per-node-pair acceptance constant, and we
measured that the accumulated guarantee `cert_err` grows with system size at fixed `τ`
(2.8e-10 → 9.2e-9 Ha from 3 to 12 atoms at level 0), so this definition of the scheme has
no clean scaling limit — to stay inside a fixed error the budget per pair must shrink as
pairs accumulate, and because the quantity being bounded decays only as a power of the
inter-block distance, the certified cutoff can then grow only like a fractional power of
`N`, which puts the pair count back near quadratic. We did not measure that exponent and
we are not claiming it. What we claim is the finite-size measurement: at the accuracy this
decomposition is used for, on chains up to 12 atoms and grids up to 30,000 points, every one
of the 33 timed screened/exact pairs is slower, by 1.08-10.28× in the archived window and
1.37-13.29× in the second. Per tolerance, over all rows at that tolerance, τ = 1e-12 Ha
costs 1.95-10.28× and 2.63-13.29×; restricted to the eight τ = 1e-12 rows that meet the
1e-6 kcal/mol target it costs 1.95-10.85×. The figure
(`figs/fig3_screening_cost.png`) therefore plots wall clock against far point pairs
*actually evaluated*, per system, which makes the statement "for the same amount of work
the screened path takes longer" checkable without any appeal to a limit.

## 7. What would have to be true for this to pay

Stated as conditions we did not meet, so the reader can check them against their own
system before implementing anything:

1. **The bound must clear the target at coarse granularity.** At atom-pair granularity the
   *smallest* block-pair bound we found in a real dimer is 197.96 Ha against a quantity of
   −8.34e-4 Ha, with `n_pairs_bound_below_value = 0` and `dropped_pairs = []`
   [`evidence/fastpair_ab/fastpair_ab_compare.json`, `ide_full` and `ide_tau1em8` rows of
   the built-geometry dimer]. The comparison sidecar
   [`evidence/fastpair_ab/fastpair_ide_validation.json`] carries the same 197.96 Ha as
   `min_block_bound_ha` and `n_dropped_screen = 0`, but neither of the two fields just named,
   and its copy of the bound differs in the last digits (…49376 against …48444 Ha). No
   traversal can fix a bound that is loose by 2.4e5×; it can only descend, and descent is
   what costs.
2. **Most pairs must be droppable at the target accuracy.** At the coarsest τ in the sweep
   that meets `|dE| ≤ 1e-6` kcal/mol, the screened path still evaluates 84.4-95.4% of the
   far point pairs (droppable share 4.6-15.6%) — and for two of the six systems (12 atoms at
   level 0, 9 atoms at level 1) no τ in the sweep meets the target at all, so their
   admissible τ is finer and the share smaller.
3. **The per-pair decision must be cheaper than the arithmetic it avoids.** Measured false:
   the tree is cheaper than the large gemm on **0 of 15** root pairs, and the near field is
   100% of the cross point pairs (all 15 root box gaps are exactly 0.0000 Å, printed by the
   root-separation check in `evidence/fastpair_ab/20260929T105823Z_pytest_fastpair_L2_L4.out`).
4. **The system must have a far field at all.** Our ladder is a chain at fixed density;
   compact dimers (S22) have no inter-block region that is both distant and negligible.
   A 300-atom protein-ligand complex with genuinely separated fragments is outside what we
   measured, and we make no claim about it.

Condition 1 is structural for VV10 as implemented in PySCF: the kernel's `g` is
`w0(r_j) R² + κ(r_i)`, and `w0` contains `(4π/3)ρ` under a square root, so a *low-density*
grid point carries a small `w0` and a huge bound. The tail points that inflate the boxes are
the same points that fail to make the bound decay. That coupling is why the atom-block
bound is loose, why refinement is the only mechanism that fires, and why refinement does
not pay.
