Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "Pair Attribution of the VV10 Nonlocal-Correlation Term
and Its Grid-Partition Dependence", for publication as an Article in JCTC.

Production codes evaluate the VV10 term as a folded per-point energy. The pair index that
defines the double sum is gone by the time the kernel returns, and we could not find a
published implementation that resolves the term into fragment pairs. We kept the index,
summed the pairs explicitly, and assigned each grid point to a fragment through a real-space
partition. At omega B97X-V/6-31G* on the eight S22 complexes, the inter-fragment block
carries 0.92-1.08 of the frozen-density superposition difference. The attribution itself
depends on the partition, though: the four Becke-family schemes spread by 0.004-0.247
kcal/mol, or 0.8-18.2% of the Becke value, with no ordering by interaction type. For the two
benzene-dimer orientations the fragment-resolved percentage differs by a factor of eleven,
while the total interaction energies rank one way before counterpoise and the other way
after it. We report both that partition dependence and a way to estimate it per system.

We also report a negative result in the main text. A certified blocking scheme for the pair
sum was timed against the exact sum: across 33 screened/exact pairs in two independent
windows, none ran faster, because the certificate available at atom-pair granularity is
loose by about 2.4e5 relative to the quantity it screens.

ORCA 6.0.1 reproduces the nonlocal differences to under 1% of the term on all eight
systems, binding energies are checked against CCSD(T)/6-31G*, and every number quoted in
the paper is mapped to an archived output. The code (nlcsplit, Apache-2.0) is public at
https://github.com/Haangyeon/nlcsplit.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.
