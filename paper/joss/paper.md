# Summary

`nlcsplit` evaluates the VV10 nonlocal-correlation energy of a Kohn-Sham density as an
explicit double sum over pairs of integration grid points, and attributes each grid point to
atoms and fragments through a real-space partition. The nonlocal term therefore separates
into intra-fragment and inter-fragment pair blocks that add back to the total: over the five
partitions and two repeated schemes of an archived water-dimer run the block-additivity
residual is 1.73e-18 to 6.94e-18 Ha. The package is pure Python and NumPy [@harris2020numpy]
on top of PySCF [@sun2018pyscf; @sun2020pyscf], is released under Apache-2.0, contains no
GAMESS source, and reproduces every number it documents from a single named script whose
standard output is archived with a hash manifest. API details live in the package
documentation rather than here.

# Statement of need

Functionals that pair a range-separated hybrid with a VV10 nonlocal term, ωB97X-V
[@mardirossian2014wb97xv] and ωB97M-V [@mardirossian2016wb97mv] above all, are a routine
choice for non-covalent interactions. The quantity itself,

$$
E_{\mathrm{NL}} = \frac{1}{2}\iint \rho(\mathbf r)\,\rho(\mathbf r')\,\Phi(\mathbf r,\mathbf r')\,d^3r\,d^3r',
$$

is a two-centre object. Production codes evaluate it by collapsing the pair sum into
per-point values, which discards the pair index and returns one number per grid point or one
total. Energy decomposition schemes can resolve the semilocal part of an interaction energy
into electrostatics, exchange, polarisation and charge transfer, but the nonlocal contribution
travels as an undivided block. A user who wants to know how much of a dispersion-corrected
attraction sits *between* the fragments has no tool for it.

The gap is one of capability, not of accuracy. On a fixed grid and a converged density our
explicit pair sum agrees with PySCF's own native VV10 kernel to 1.0e-08 and 1.5e-08 Ha on the
two archived settings, so nothing is gained by questioning the total energy; what is missing
is the index.

# State of the field

VV10 [@vydrov2009vv10] and the broader vdW-DF family are usually discussed through the total
energy or through the shape of the pair kernel itself
[@hyldgaard2014interp; @dion2004]. The closest published treatment of the term in an
interaction-energy context computes exactly that undivided block and asks whether it is
accurate [@dasgupta2023]; it does not resolve it into fragment pairs. The closest published
explicit evaluation of the double sum, JuNoLo [@lazic2010junolo], post-processes a
converged density in parallel to obtain the total correlation energy, and its own text
recommends the method for systems "where one cannot tell apart fragments" - the case
complementary to the one this package addresses. The real-space partitions we reuse, Becke's
proatom assignment [@becke1988] and the Stratmann-Scuseria-Frisch weighting
[@stratmann1996], are long established for semilocal decomposition but had not, in the work
we found, been applied to the nonlocal pair sum.

This is a screen rather than an enumeration. It rests on recorded database searches whose own
log states that open-index *subject* searching has unreliable recall, so we claim only that no
published fragment-resolved evaluation of this term was found, not that none exists. The
benchmark context is the GMTKN55/S22 set [@goerigk2017gmtkn55; @jurecka2006s22].

# Software design

Four decisions carry most of the behaviour, and each has an observable consequence.

**Conventions are read, not hard-coded.** The range-separation parameters (b, C), the
one-body coefficient β and the fields w0 and κ come from PySCF's own `nlc_coeff`. The
pairing convention `g = w0(r_i)R² + κ(r_i)` - same-centre, both factors taken at the same
grid point - was selected because it is the one that reproduces the native kernel. The
cross-paired variant is retained as a definition-sensitivity control rather than deleted, and
it is not a harmless re-indexing: it shifts monomer energies by +0.1379 (water), +0.4182
(argon) and +0.1346 (methane) kcal/mol at grid level 1 and +0.9057 for benzene at level 0, the
levels the producing script itself selects.

**Only the double sum is decomposed.** Because β is a constant and ∫ρ is the electron number,
the local term β∫ρ = βN is exactly one-body and cancels identically from any superposition
difference. An inter-fragment decomposition therefore needs no convention for splitting a
local energy density - a property of the pair sum alone. The cost is stated rather than
hidden: the absolute local term of one fragment is β·N_A, so its spread across partition
schemes is 3.0415 kcal/mol per electron, 0.329 kcal/mol for the water dimer on the official
S22 geometry.

**Attribution is audited against the host program, not against itself.** The partition
interface returns a point-by-fragment factor matrix normalised to unity at every point, and
refuses to return unless its weighted grid reproduces the weights PySCF's own
`get_partition` produces, reached through `libdft` over ctypes. That anchor is deliberately
external: checking a re-implementation against itself would not be evidence.

**The density floor is an implementation detail.** Points below ρ = 1e-12 are dropped from
the pair sum; sweeping the floor from 1e-8 to 1e-16 moves the inter-fragment term by at
most 2.1e-11 Ha at grid level 1 and 4.2e-11 Ha at level 2 - which the run's log prints as
0.000000 in kcal/mol - while discarding 10.7-22.6% of the points and at most 2.3e-6 electrons,
so users are not asked to tune it.

On the reporting side, the package applies one rule to its own output: where
|ΔE_NLC/ΔE_xc| > 2 the two parts carry opposite signs or the denominator cancels, and the
code reports the two absolute terms and refuses to state a share. An export-time gate applies
the same rule to the prose. Fifteen gates run on every rebuild, each with a bidirectional
self-test, and six of them exist because they caught a real defect in this release rather than
a planted one. The suite collects 69 tests; all 69 pass in the source tree under both PySCF
versions in the CI matrix (2.12.1 and 2.14.0) and pass again from outside the tree against the
installed package, where one test skips by design because the benchmark geometry it needs is
not part of the install. The distributed wheel carries 49 of the 69; the 20 that stay behind
belong to a frozen control implementation that is deliberately not distributed, and shipping
its test without it would be a collection-time ImportError.

# Research impact

The package turns three open questions into numbers, and one number into a negative result.

*How much of the nonlocal correlation is inter-fragment?* On the six S22 complexes that carry
a counterpoise reference at grid level 2, the inter-fragment block accounts for 0.92-1.08 of
the frozen-density superposition difference, so association-time nonlocal correlation is
carried almost entirely by point pairs straddling the fragments.

*How much does the partition choice matter?* It supplies a per-system error bar for any future
use of such numbers: the attribution span runs 0.0040-0.2470 kcal/mol absolute across the
eight complexes in the cross-program table, with no ordering by interaction type or system
size, and it is not basis-invariant - 0.0249 kcal/mol for the water dimer in STO-3G against
0.0382-0.0400 in four larger sets.

*Is the decomposed quantity right?* A second program decides. On the same geometries, basis
and counterpoise convention, ORCA 6.0.1's independently implemented nonlocal term differs
from ours by 0.0004-0.0416 kcal/mol over eight systems (0.067-0.952% relative), and in seven
of the eight the cross-program residual is smaller than the spread produced by the partition
choice - the attribution convention, not the program, dominates. The same two programs
reproduce a coupled-cluster anchor to within 0.0002 kcal/mol over 24 single points, with the
AO space checked as identical for every one of them.

Finally, the machinery falsified a plausible optimisation. A pair block with a provable upper
bound should be skippable, and the screening path built from that bound was timed against the
exact sum in two independent windows, 33 screened/exact pairs in total. None was faster: 1.08-
10.28× slower in the first window and 1.37-13.29× in the second, recomputed here from the
archived wall-clock pairs. Of those 33 runs, the eight that actually meet the stated
1e-6 kcal/mol target still evaluate 84.4-95.4% of the far-field point pairs, which is why the
bound buys nothing. The paper reports this as a cost, not as a benefit.
